# Artifact archetypes

## Contents

- Internal workbooks
- Internal memos and notes
- Email and correspondence
- Policies and agreements
- System exports and registers

## Internal workbooks

Start with the workflow population, not the final calculation. Separate raw exports, mapping, calculations, review, and presentation only where the owner would. Plausible depth includes prior-period data, inactive rows, exception flags, source IDs, account mappings, bridge tabs, checks, comments, print setup, and bounded side work.

The workbook fails realism when it contains only the rows needed for the task, every tab shares one presentation style, or a scratch tab exists solely to look human.

## Internal memos and notes

Use the length and structure the author needs: background, decision, options, risks, sources, appendices, and unresolved follow-up. Working copies may carry a few review marks or copied-forward conventions. Do not turn every memo into a polished executive brief.

## Email and correspondence

Choose native message, thread, printout, or scanned retained copy. Include quoted context, recipients, timestamps, attachment references, partial replies, forwarding, or shorthand only as the lifecycle supports. Different participants should not write with identical structure and completeness.

## Policies and agreements

Depth comes from scope, definitions, ownership, controls, exceptions, revision history, boilerplate, schedules, and execution blocks. External records remain cleaner than internal workpapers. Do not compress an executed agreement into a two-page task summary.

## System exports and registers

Preserve system field order, codes, null behavior, identifiers, timestamps, repetition, and awkwardness. Human intervention belongs in editable fields or later review layers, not as a redesign of the export.
